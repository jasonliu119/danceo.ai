# How to Choose Inputs for an AI Dance Video

A successful photo-to-dance video starts with a recognizable subject and a reference whose motion is easy to read. The image supplies appearance; the video guides motion and composition.

## Choose the image

- Use one prominent subject with a clear face and visible body.
- Favor good lighting, sharp detail, and contrast against the background.
- Avoid motion blur, heavy occlusion, and crops that hide the body needed for the move.
- Use a photo or original character image you have permission to transform.

A front-facing or three-quarter view is a useful starting point. For Danceo, the published guide lists JPG, PNG, and WebP images up to 10 MB; confirm current requirements in the generator.

## Choose the movement reference

Start with a short, authorized clip with a single visible dancer. Look for a stable camera, consistent framing, and a sequence of distinct gestures. A reference with rapid cuts or a subject that repeatedly leaves the frame can be harder to interpret.

Match the reference to the image: a full-body dance is easier to evaluate when the source character's body is also visible. Browse examples for inspiration and upload a reference you are allowed to use.

## Iterate with a purpose

If the character is hard to recognize, try a sharper image or a simpler background. If the movement feels unclear, try a shorter reference with fewer overlapping gestures. Change one input at a time so you can tell which change helped. Results vary by input and generation.
